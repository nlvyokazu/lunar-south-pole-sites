Limits and sources

Scope: Geometry only. No power, thermal, panel or antenna model.

What this tool computes

Hourly Sun and Earth positions for 365 days starting 2026-11-15 00:00 UTC at three fixed sites, compared with a terrain horizon for each site. Geometry only: no power, thermal, panel or antenna model. Results say whether a site meets the thresholds you set, nothing about whether a mission could use it.

Definitions

Sun-visible
At least half of the Sun's disk is above the terrain horizon. The Sun's apparent angular radius is computed per hour.
Earth-in-view
The center of the Earth is above the terrain horizon.
Horizon margin
The horizon margin slider raises the horizon by the chosen number of degrees for both bodies.
Where each result is computed
Sun-visible hours are computed by a C++ kernel compiled to WebAssembly in the browser when it loads. If the module does not load, the margin slider is disabled and the page uses the precomputed Sun-visible values at margin 0. Earth-in-view is computed in TypeScript.
Windows at the range edges
Windows that touch the start or end of the selected range are shown with a leading >= because the true length is longer.

Data sources

  • Positions: NASA NAIF SPICE kernels DE440s (planetary ephemeris), naif0012.tls (leap seconds), moon_pa_de440_200625.bpc and moon_de440_250416.tf (Moon orientation, mean Earth frame), read with spiceypy.
  • Terrain: LRO LOLA LDEM_75S_120M polar digital elevation model, 120 m per pixel, from the Imbrium mirror at imbrium.mit.edu of the PDS product.
  • Horizon masks are scanned out to 150 km at 0.5 degree azimuth steps.
  • Compiler: Emscripten 6.0.11.

Limitations

  • Samples are hourly, so sunset and Earthset times are accurate to within the hour.
  • No atmospheric refraction is applied (the Moon has none that matters here).
  • The Sun's disk is tested against the horizon height at the Sun's center azimuth.
  • Earth passing in front of the Sun is not modelled.
  • Aberration is applied at the Moon's center only.
  • Only the three fixed sites are available, with no custom coordinates.
  • The terrain model has 120 m pixels. For Malapert A and Nobile the highest nearby terrain is about one pixel from the site, so those two horizons depend on the exact site position. This sensitivity has not been tested.
  • The terrain model's frame label (DE421 mean Earth) differs slightly from the frame used for positions (DE440 mean Earth). The offset is believed to be under 1 arcsecond but was not verified here.
  • The numbers come directly from the SPICE kernels and have not been compared with JPL Horizons output by this project.
  • The Amundsen site coordinates (88.5 S, 124.0 E) were supplied by the team and have not been checked against a published crater position, so read the label as “near Amundsen”.
