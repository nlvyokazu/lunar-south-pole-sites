Limits and sources

Scope: Geometry only. No power, thermal, panel or antenna model.

What this tool computes

Hourly Sun and Earth positions for 365 days starting 2026-11-15 00:00 UTC at three fixed sites, compared with a terrain horizon for each site. Results say whether a site meets the thresholds you set. They say nothing about whether a mission could use it.

Definitions

Sun-visible
At least half of the Sun's disk is above the terrain horizon. The Sun's apparent angular radius is computed per hour.
Earth-in-view
The center of the Earth is above the terrain horizon.
Horizon margin
The horizon margin slider raises the horizon by the chosen number of degrees for both bodies.
Rank
Rank only orders the three sites by fixed rules: sites that meet all thresholds first, then fewer failed checks, then higher Sun-visible percentage, then higher Earth-in-view percentage. It is not a recommendation, and all three sites can fail every threshold.
Windows at the range edges
Windows that touch the start or end of the selected range are shown with a leading >= because the true length is longer. A site cannot pass a maximum-length limit on such a window alone.

Where the calculations run

  • In your browser, a C++ engine compiled to WebAssembly computes Sun-disc visibility, Sun and Earth flags, windows, percentages, threshold checks, ranking and the next sunset and Earthset.
  • The page code, written in TypeScript, reads your inputs, formats dates and draws the table.
  • When the page starts, the engine output at margin 0 is checked against the precomputed data. If the engine does not load or the check fails, the page says so, disables the margin slider, and uses TypeScript copies of the same logic with the precomputed Sun values at margin 0.
  • The precomputed data is made offline by Python scripts, not by C++. Those scripts read the SPICE kernels and the terrain model once. The page then loads the result as a fixed file.

Data sources

  • Positions: NASA NAIF SPICE kernels de440s.bsp (planetary ephemeris), naif0012.tls (leap seconds), moon_pa_de440_200625.bpc and moon_de440_250416.tf (Moon orientation, mean Earth frame), read with spiceypy.
  • Terrain: LRO LOLA LDEM_75S_120M polar digital elevation model, 120 m per pixel, from the Imbrium mirror at imbrium.mit.edu of the NASA PDS product.
  • Horizon masks are scanned out to 150 km at 0.5 degree azimuth steps.
  • Compiler: Emscripten 6.0.11.

Limitations

  • Samples are hourly, so sunset and Earthset times are accurate to within the hour.
  • No atmospheric refraction is applied.
  • The Sun's disk is tested against the horizon height at the Sun's center azimuth.
  • Earth passing in front of the Sun is not modelled.
  • Aberration is applied at the Moon's center only. Site rotation is not modelled.
  • Only the three fixed sites are available, with no custom coordinates.
  • The terrain model has 120 m pixels. For Malapert A and Nobile the highest nearby terrain is about one pixel from the site, so those two horizons depend on the exact site position. This sensitivity has not been tested.
  • The terrain model's frame label (DE421 mean Earth) differs from the frame used for positions (DE440 mean Earth). The size of the resulting offset has not been quantified here.
  • The numbers come directly from the SPICE kernels and have not been compared with JPL Horizons output by this project.
  • All three site coordinates were supplied by the team, are unverified, and are treated as mean Earth coordinates. The Amundsen point (88.5 S, 124.0 E) has not been checked against a published crater position, so read the label as “near Amundsen”.

AI use

Some code in this project was written with SI chat assistants, including Claude and ChatGPT. That covers some of the C++ engine, the TypeScript page, the Python data scripts and the tests. The team chose the scope and the three sites, and ran every command and test on its own machine. Because the engine, the TypeScript model and the tests were all written to the same specification, the tests show that they agree with each other and with the precomputed data. They are not an independent check of the astronomy.The code has been fully reviewed.
